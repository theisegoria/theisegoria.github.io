1. Gradient descent and conditioning

Follow gradient steps across elliptical contours. Strongly unequal curvatures make steps zigzag. Newton’s exact step reaches this quadratic minimum in one iteration.

Worked example. For κ=4, constant-step gradient descent converges when 0<η<0.5.

Watch out. Newton’s one-step result is special to an invertible quadratic Hessian. It is not a general guarantee.

Why can increasing the step size make progress worse?

An update multiplier can have magnitude above one, amplifying errors.

Reference: Boyd and Vandenberghe · Convex Optimization

2. A moving constraint

Move the line x+y=b across concentric objective contours. The optimum is where the smallest reachable circle touches the line.

Worked example. For b=2, the best point is (1,1), with objective value 2.

Watch out. This is an equality constraint. Replacing it with an inequality changes the feasible region and sometimes the optimum.

Why must x and y be equal at the optimum?

Because x²+y²=b²/2+(x-y)²/2, minimized at x=y.

Reference: Boyd and Vandenberghe · Convex Optimization

3. Multipliers and sensitivity

The optimal value curve tells us how costly a change in the constraint is. The tangent slope equals the multiplier when the Lagrangian uses λ(b-x-y).

Worked example. At b=2, increasing b by 0.01 raises the optimum by about 0.02.

Watch out. Multiplier signs depend on the chosen constraint convention. Sensitivity is a local approximation.

What is the duality gap at λ=b?

Zero: the primal minimum and dual maximum both equal b²/2.

Reference: Boyd and Vandenberghe · Convex Optimization
