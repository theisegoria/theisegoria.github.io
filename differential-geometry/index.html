Differential geometry: curvature and shape

Metrics, geodesics, and curvature on surfaces.

Before you begin: Calculus and vectors

Predict, manipulate, then check your reasoning against the example and question. Graphs illustrate the mathematics; they do not replace a proof.

1. Curvature of a curve

Change the bend of a planar curve and inspect its signed curvature.

Worked example. A circle of radius \(R\) has constant curvature \(1/R\).

Watch out. The visual uses a smooth teaching curve, not a measured path.

Where is \(\kappa\) largest?

Where the tangent direction changes fastest with arc length.

Reference: MIT OpenCourseWare · Differential Geometry

2. Geodesics on a surface

Compare a straight coordinate path with a shortest path drawn across a curved surface.

Worked example. Geodesics locally minimize distance when no conjugate point intervenes.

Watch out. A map projection can make a geodesic look curved or straight.

What does the connection correct?

It subtracts coordinate artefacts from ordinary acceleration.

Reference: MIT OpenCourseWare · Differential Geometry

3. Gaussian curvature

Morph between a bowl and a saddle and compare intrinsic curvature signs.

Worked example. A sphere has positive Gaussian curvature \(K>0\); a saddle has negative curvature \(K<0\).

Watch out. The plotted surfaces are illustrative parameterized patches.

Can bending without stretching change \(K\)?

For an isometric bend, intrinsic Gaussian curvature is preserved.

Reference: MIT OpenCourseWare · Differential Geometry
