Information theory: uncertainty and codes

Entropy, noisy channels, and information distance.

Before you begin: Probability and logarithms

Predict, manipulate, then check your reasoning against the example and question. Graphs illustrate the mathematics; they do not replace a proof.

1. Entropy

Tune a binary source and see uncertainty peak at a fair coin.

Worked example. A fair binary source carries one bit per symbol.

Watch out. Entropy measures uncertainty in a distribution, not the meaning of a message.

When is binary entropy maximal?

At p=1/2.

Reference: Cover and Thomas · Elements of Information Theory

2. A noisy channel

Increase bit-flip noise and compare input and output uncertainty.

Worked example. A noiseless binary channel has one bit of capacity.

Watch out. Capacity is an asymptotic coding limit, not the success rate of one short message.

What happens at a flip probability of one half?

The output is independent of the input and mutual information is zero.

Reference: Cover and Thomas · Elements of Information Theory

3. Divergence

Compare a model distribution with observations using Kullback–Leibler divergence.

Worked example. D_KL is zero only when the two distributions agree.

Watch out. It is not symmetric and is not a metric.

Why can a tiny Q(x) be costly?

Underestimating an event that occurs makes log(P/Q) large.

Reference: Cover and Thomas · Elements of Information Theory
