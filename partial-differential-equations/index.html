Partial differential equations: fields in motion

Heat, waves, and potential fields on a grid.

Before you begin: Calculus and vectors

1. Heat diffusion2. Wave equation3. Potential fields

Predict, manipulate, then check your reasoning against the example and question. Graphs illustrate the mathematics; they do not replace a proof.

1. Heat diffusion

Scrub time as a hot spot spreads and smooths across a one-dimensional rod.

Worked example. Diffusion reduces sharp gradients while conserving total heat under insulated boundaries.

Watch out. The finite difference step must obey a stability bound.

Why does the peak fall?

Heat flows down the gradient into neighbouring cells.

Reference: MIT OpenCourseWare · Partial Differential Equations

2. Wave equation

Change tension and watch a pulse reflect from fixed ends.

Worked example. A fixed end reverses the sign of the reflected displacement.

Watch out. The string model ignores damping and transverse nonlinearities.

What controls wave speed?

The parameter c, set by tension and linear density.

Reference: MIT OpenCourseWare · Partial Differential Equations

3. Potential fields

Move two charged boundaries and inspect equipotential contours and field lines.

Worked example. Field lines cross equipotentials at right angles.

Watch out. The plotted solution is a teaching model on a finite rectangular domain.

Where is the field strongest?

Where equipotentials are packed most closely.

Reference: MIT OpenCourseWare · Partial Differential Equations
