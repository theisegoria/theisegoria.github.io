# Bony pelvis, walls, floor, fascia, vessels, and nerves

## The pelvic ring
### Hip bone: ilium, ischium, pubis fused at acetabulum
### Symphysis anteriorly, sacroiliac joints posteriorly
#### Closed ring: one break, look for another
### Brim separates greater from lesser pelvis
#### Inlet in plane of brim
#### Outlet: pubic arch, tuberosities, sacrotuberous, coccyx
### Sacrospinous, sacrotuberous ligaments
#### Convert notches into foramina
#### Resist sacral rotation
### Sacroiliac joint: synovial front, posterior syndesmosis
#### Weight wedges sacrum between ilia
### Ring injury by force and stability
#### AP compression opens symphysis and sacroiliac
#### Lateral compression buckles hemipelvis
#### Vertical shear disrupts posterior support
#### Bleeding from bone, venous plexuses, arteries
#### Assess urethra, bladder, rectum, plexus, vessels
### Symphysis: secondary cartilaginous joint
#### Pregnancy widening modest, trauma destabilises

## Dimensions and variation
### Inlet, midpelvis, outlet influence childbirth
#### No single measurement predicts birth mechanics
#### Moulding, position, soft tissue, uterine force
### Sex-associated average differences overlap
#### Age, ancestry, stature, activity, nutrition
#### Answer a clinical question, not identity
### Obstetric conjugate not directly measurable
#### Diagonal conjugate estimates it
### Interspinous distance: midpelvic transverse

## Lateral and posterior walls
### Lateral: obturator membrane, obturator internus
#### Obturator canal to medial thigh
### Posterior: sacrum, coccyx, piriformis, ligaments
### Piriformis divides greater sciatic foramen
#### Superior gluteal vessels and nerve above
#### Sciatic, inferior gluteal, pudendal below
### Pudendal nerve curves round ischial spine
#### Enables nerve block
#### Vulnerable in childbirth and surgery

## Pelvic diaphragm
### Levator ani and coccygeus with fascia
### Puborectalis, pubococcygeus, iliococcygeus
### Puborectalis sling at anorectal junction
#### Keeps anorectal angle at rest
#### Relaxes during defecation
### Levator supports viscera, resists pressure
### Hiatus: urethra, vagina, anorectal canal
#### Necessary weak region, changes with loading
### Sacral plexus branches, pudendal to sphincters
### Excess tone: pain, voiding difficulty
### Weakness: prolapse, incontinence

## Endopelvic fascia and support
### Parietal fascia on walls, visceral on organs
### Loose planes permit filling and dissection
#### Also transmit vessels and spread
### Cardinal, uterosacral: cervix and upper vagina
### Puboprostatic, pubovesical: bladder neck
### Perineal body: central fibromuscular node
#### Injury weakens posterior wall and continence
### Prolapse: multi-component support failure
#### Descent does not map perfectly to symptoms
#### Repair integrated support, not one defect

## Internal iliac vessels
### Posterior division: iliolumbar, lateral sacral, superior gluteal
### Anterior division: viscera, perineum, gluteal region
### Extensive anastomoses across midline
### Uterine artery crosses above ureter
### Corona mortis crosses superior pubic ramus
### Valveless venous plexuses
#### Communicate with vertebral plexus
#### Collapse poorly, bleeding diffuse

## Somatic and autonomic nerves
### Sacral plexus on piriformis
#### Masses, childbirth, surgery affect many nerves
### Sympathetics via hypogastric plexuses
### Pelvic splanchnic nerves S2 to S4, parasympathetic
### Pudendal nerve S2 to S4, somatic
#### Do not confuse despite shared roots
### Pelvic pain line
#### Above: sympathetic, thoracolumbar levels
#### Below: parasympathetic, sacral levels

## Pelvic spaces
### Retropubic space: surgical access
### Paravesical, pararectal: vessels, nerves, ureters
### Presacral: autonomic plexus, vessels
### Extraperitoneal bleeding spreads widely
### Infection and tumour follow fascial planes
### Imaging defines sidewall, floor, peritoneum, ureter
