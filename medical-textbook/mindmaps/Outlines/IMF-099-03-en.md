# Stress, motivation, and behaviour change

## Stress and its physiology
### Stressor is the event, stress the response
### Threat, challenge, loss, or irrelevance
### Acute stress prioritises attention and energy
### Harm: extreme, prolonged, poor recovery
### Rapid sympathetic and adrenal-medullary response
#### Noradrenaline: alertness, vascular tone
#### Adrenaline: cardiac output, ventilation, glucose
### Slower hypothalamic-pituitary-adrenal axis
#### Cortisol mobilises substrates, alters immunity
#### Feeds back to brain and pituitary
### One cortisol value needs timing and context

## Allostasis and appraisal
### Allostasis: stability through change
### Allostatic load: cumulative physiological cost
#### A framework, not a standard clinical test
### Poverty, discrimination, caregiving sustain demand
#### Not reducible to individual attitude
### Primary appraisal: what is at stake
### Secondary appraisal: options and coping resources

## Coping, control, and support
### Problem-, emotion-, meaning-focused coping
### Direct action if controllable, acceptance if not
### Avoidance relieves briefly, maintains fear
### Perceived control helps unless illusory or blaming
### Predictability permits preparation
### Support quality matters more than contact
### Advice ignoring autonomy becomes a demand

## Motivation and expectancy
### Drives, incentives, and goals
### Dopamine: learning reward predictors, not pleasure
### Wanting, liking, effort, learning dissociate
#### Cue pull without pleasure in addiction
### Expectancy-value: success, value, cost
### Risk information changes one component
### Ask which immediate costs dominate

## Self-efficacy and autonomy
### Self-efficacy: confidence for a specific behaviour
#### Practice, modelling, encouragement, physiology
### Not generic self-esteem
### Specific attainable goals with feedback
### Break broad goals into observable steps
### Autonomous versus controlled motivation
### Competence, autonomy, relatedness
### Controlling rewards undermine interest
### Informed choice is not abandonment

## Habits and planning
### Cue-linked responses from stable repetition
### Persist after the outcome loses value
### Knowledge change alone has limited effect
### Redesign cues, friction, sequence, reinforcement
### Implementation intention links cue to action
### Action planning: where, when, how
### Coping planning: response to barriers
### Plans must stay flexible

## Behaviour models and conversation
### Capability, opportunity, motivation
### Keeps structural barriers in view
### Training, reminders, redesign match gaps
### Stage models match support to readiness
#### Progress is not always in order
### Motivational interviewing explores ambivalence
#### Elicits own reasons, avoids argument

## Risk and behavioural economics
### Immediacy, vividness, dread, trust shape risk
### Relative risk without baseline misleads
### Absolute frequencies with the same denominator
### Check understanding in the person's own words
### Defaults, present bias, loss aversion, norms
### Ethical defaults: transparent, easy refusal
### Incentives can coerce when resources are scarce

## Relapse, evaluation, and synthesis
### Relapse is information, not moral failure
### Lapse: review, self-compassion, restart
### Stress narrows attention, strengthens habits
### Lifestyle cannot remove structural adversity
### Specify mechanism and outcome
### Engagement is not benefit
### Monitor stigma and widening disparities
### Smallest mechanism-matched intervention, then revise
