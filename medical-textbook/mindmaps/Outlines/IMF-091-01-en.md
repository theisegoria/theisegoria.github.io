# DNA replication, telomeres, and repair

## Copying principles
### Antiparallel strands, five-prime-to-three-prime synthesis
#### Extension only from a free three-prime hydroxyl
### Semiconservative: old strand templates new
### Pairing, active-site geometry, proofreading
#### Residual errors corrected after synthesis
### Genome maintenance is a network

## Origins and the fork
### Thousands of origins across chromosomes
### Licensing in late mitosis and gap one
#### Inactive helicases loaded
### Synthesis-phase kinases fire a subset
#### Activation blocks relicensing: copied once
### Dormant origins rescue stalled regions
### Helicase unwinds, single-strand binders protect
### Topoisomerases cut and reseal to relieve strain
### Primase makes RNA-DNA primers
#### Replicative polymerases cannot start de novo

## Strand synthesis and chromatin restoration
### Leading strand continuous toward fork
### Lagging strand as Okazaki fragments
#### Primers removed, gaps filled, ligase seals
### Polymerase epsilon leading, delta lagging
### PCNA sliding clamp gives processivity
#### Clamp loaders use ATP
### Histones recycled and new ones deposited
### Epigenetic marks re-established by reader-writers

## Fork stress
### Lesions, bound proteins, transcription, low nucleotides
### Checkpoint kinases stabilise forks
#### Restrain origin firing and delay cycle
### Fork reversal protects ends
### Recombination restarts collapsed forks
### Unresolved stress: breaks and rearrangements
#### Major source of cancer evolution

## Telomeres
### Repetitive DNA-protein caps
### Incomplete end copying and nucleolytic shortening
### Shelterin stops ends looking like breaks
### Critical dysfunction: checkpoints and fusions
#### Breakage-fusion cycles
### Telomerase: reverse transcriptase with RNA template
#### Germ cells, stem cells, lymphocytes, most cancers
### Alternative lengthening by recombination
### Shortening limits replication, maintenance immortalises

## Lesions and early correction
### Lesion is chemistry, mutation is fixed sequence
### Repair restores, tolerates, or introduces errors
### Proofreading exonuclease acts immediately
### Mismatch repair after replication
#### Excises a stretch of the new strand
### Lynch syndrome
#### Microsatellite instability
#### Screen tumours, then interpret the cause

## Excision and direct repair
### Base excision for small non-distorting lesions
#### Glycosylase leaves an abasic site
#### Endonuclease, processing, fill, ligate
### PARP detects single-strand breaks
#### Homologous-repair-deficient tumours: synthetic lethality
### Nucleotide excision for bulky distortions
#### Global-genome versus transcription-coupled
### Xeroderma pigmentosum: skin and eye cancer
### Cockayne spectrum: growth and neurological disease
### Direct reversal: MGMT suicide reaction
#### Silencing raises alkylating-agent sensitivity

## Double-strand breaks and crosslinks
### Homologous recombination uses sister chromatid
#### Favoured in synthesis and gap two
#### Resection, RAD51 filament, strand invasion
### BRCA variants impair homologous repair
#### Genomic scars, secondary mutations restore
### Classical end joining throughout the cycle
#### Essential for antigen-receptor rearrangement
#### May leave small insertions or deletions
### Alternative end joining uses microhomology
### Crosslinks need nucleases, bypass, recombination
#### Fanconi: marrow failure, anomalies, cancer
