# Inflammation, repair, fibrosis, and environmental injury

## Purpose of inflammation
### Recognise danger, contain cause, prepare repair
### Pathological when excessive, prolonged, unresolved
### Inflammation is not the same as infection
#### Sterile infarction, crystals, trauma, tumours

## Acute vascular and cellular events
### Minutes to days: vessels, proteins, neutrophils
### Sentinel cells detect danger patterns
### Arteriolar dilation causes warmth and redness
### Permeability leaks protein-rich fluid
#### Swelling and slowed, concentrated flow
### Leukocytes marginate, roll, adhere, migrate
### Neutrophils arrive early
#### Granules, oxidants, proteases, traps
#### Kill microbes but damage host
### Monocytes become macrophages

## Exudates and mediators
### Serous: cell-poor fluid
### Fibrinous: fibrin may organise into adhesions
### Suppurative: pus
#### Abscess may need drainage
### Ulcer: lost epithelium over inflammation
### Histamine: rapid dilation and leak
### Prostaglandins and leukotrienes
### Complement opsonises and recruits
### Tumour necrosis factor and interleukin one
### Blocking one pathway can impair defence

## Systemic response and resolution
### Fever via hypothalamic prostaglandins
#### Chills generate heat to new set point
### Acute-phase proteins rise, albumin falls
### Severe activation causes leak and coagulopathy
### Resolution is active
#### Remove stimulus, stop neutrophil recruitment
#### Macrophages clear apoptotic neutrophils
#### Failed resolution sustains injury

## Chronic and granulomatous inflammation
### Weeks to years: infiltration, destruction, repair
### Persistent infection, autoimmunity, toxins, tumours
### Macrophage programmes change with signals
### Granuloma: epithelioid and giant cells
#### Infectious and non-infectious causes
#### Caseation supportive, not specific
#### Sparse organisms need integrated testing

## Regeneration and scar formation
### Regeneration needs proliferating cells and scaffold
### Scar when injury extensive or matrix lost
### Angiogenesis builds new capillaries
### Granulation tissue is not granulomatous
### Fibroblasts deposit matrix
#### Type three collagen remodelled to type one
### Metalloproteinase balance
#### Excess degradation weakens repair
#### Excess synthesis produces fibrosis
### Myofibroblasts contract wounds

## Wound healing and complications
### Primary intention: apposed clean edges
### Secondary intention: more contraction and matrix
### Tensile strength rises slowly, rarely complete
### Impaired by infection, poor perfusion, glucocorticoids
#### Vitamin C deficiency impairs hydroxylation
### Hypertrophic scar stays within wound
### Keloid extends beyond wound
### Adhesions and contractures

## Organ fibrosis
### Final pathway in chronic organ injury
### Repeated damage activates mesenchymal cells
### Transforming growth factor beta drives matrix
### Stiffness feeds back via mechanotransduction
### Cross-linking and parenchymal loss limit reversal

## Environmental injury
### Dose, route, duration, metabolism, susceptibility
### Particle size sets deposition site
### Fibres resist clearance
### Occupational history spans a lifetime
### Metabolic activation creates reactive intermediates
### Genotoxic carcinogens: no assumed safe dose
### Ionising radiation damages DNA
#### Deterministic effects above thresholds
#### Stochastic cancer risk is probabilistic
### Thermal, cold, electrical, pressure injury
#### Electrical: deep necrosis, minor skin change

## Systemic inflammation and sepsis
### No single mediator or value diagnoses it
### Infection or sterile injury can trigger it
### Sepsis: dysregulated response with organ dysfunction
### Injury continues as microbes fall
### Defence and repair can themselves cause disease
