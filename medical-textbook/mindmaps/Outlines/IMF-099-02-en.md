# Lifespan development and growth

## Principles of development
### Genes, maturation, experience, culture, history
### Neither rigid timetable nor unlimited plasticity
### Skills emerge in ranges by several viable paths
### Assess domains, change, opportunity, function
### One delayed milestone does not identify a cause
### Loss of an established skill is more concerning

## Brain development and sensitive periods
### Proliferation, migration, differentiation, cell death
### Synapses overproduced, refined by activity
### Myelination and specialisation continue for decades
### Sensitive periods are not absolute deadlines
#### Recovery depends on timing, duration, system

## Attachment and temperament
### Infants regulate arousal through caregivers
### Attachment: secure base, not a personality label
#### Shaped by availability, temperament, culture
### Atypical patterns may be adaptation
### Temperament: reactivity, attention, self-regulation
### Goodness of fit between child and environment
#### Reactive child thrives with calm routines
### "Difficult" label treats tendency as destiny

## Motor and language development
### Motor: maturation, body, strength, practice
### Infants discover solutions to constraints
### New locomotion changes cognitive experience
### Assess vision, hearing, tone, asymmetry, pain
### Language: preparedness plus social learning
### Receptive precedes expressive
### Joint attention and gesture link focus to words
### Passive media lacks contingent feedback
### Multilingual exposure does not cause disorder

## Cognition and executive function
### Children reason well in familiar contexts
#### Fail tasks needing language or inhibition
### Stage theories underestimate early competence
### Knowledge is domain-specific
### Executive: inhibition, updating, switching, planning
### Frontoparietal, subcortical, cerebellar networks
### Performance depends on sleep, stress, environment
### Externalise demands with routines and visual cues

## Social and moral understanding
### Theory of mind represents beliefs and intentions
### False-belief task is one milestone
### Real social cognition integrates ambiguous cues
### Difference does not mean absent empathy
### Morality: emotion, learning, reasoning, institutions
### Moral harms distinguished from conventions
### Reasoning expands from punishment to principles
### Moral language does not guarantee behaviour

## Adolescence and identity
### Puberty plus expanding social and legal roles
### Reward systems respond before regulation matures
#### Especially in emotional peer contexts
### Not uniformly irrational
### Risk harmful with intense reward, low supervision
### Identity integrates values, roles, body, culture
### Exploration and commitment can be phases
### Distress not automatically due to identity

## Adulthood and ageing
### Intimacy, work, caregiving, generativity
### Processing speed declines gradually
### Knowledge, vocabulary, expertise stable or grow
### Cross-sectional cohort and longitudinal survival bias
### Normal ageing slows retrieval, not dementia
### Sensory loss masquerades as cognitive impairment
### Acute decline: delirium, illness, medication
### Narrowing future time changes priorities
### Grief varies, no mandatory stages

## Context, categories, and formulation
### Nested systems shape daily inputs
### Adversity alters stress physiology, not destiny
### Protective relationships and services buffer risk
### Resilience must not shift responsibility
### Diagnostic thresholds divide continuous traits
### Masking conceals difficulty at a cost
### Formulate profile, context, goals, accommodations
### Compare with own trajectory, not only population
