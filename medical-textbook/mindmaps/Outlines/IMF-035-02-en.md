# Microbial strategy, niches, and evolution under selection

## Architecture shapes clinical reasoning
### Architecture constrains ability and drug targets
### Fungi share eukaryotic machinery with humans
#### Narrower selective toxicity
### Viruses expose step-specific targets
### Diagnose state, niche, burden, host relationship

## Bacterial envelope and drug access
### Wall-active drugs weaken the expanding structure
#### Active growth important for killing
### Gram-positive: thick exposed wall, no outer membrane
### Gram-negative: thin wall behind outer membrane
#### Porins restrict entry, pumps and enzymes reduce exposure
#### Porin loss plus beta-lactamase: strong resistance
### Mycolic acids slow permeability, prolong treatment
### Mycoplasmas: no wall, beta-lactams fail
### Capsules inhibit complement and phagocytosis
#### Opsonising antibody restores clearance
#### Absent spleen: encapsulated bacteraemia dangerous

## Physiological state, biofilms, source control
### Abscess, phagolysosome, bone, biofilm slow growth
#### Oxygen, iron, pH alter expression and susceptibility
### Persisters: transient phenotypic survivors
#### Relapse from protected sites
### Biofilm: attachment, matrix, maturation
#### Matrix limits immune access and diffusion
#### Antibiotics fail to sterilise adherent reservoir
### Source control: drainage, debridement, device removal
### Persistent fever: reassess source before switching
### Suppressive therapy is distinct from eradication

## Viral tasks, tropism, and stability
### Positive-sense RNA acts directly as messenger
### Negative-sense viruses carry an RNA polymerase
### Retroviruses integrate, creating reservoirs
### Tropism needs more than a receptor
#### Intracellular factors, temperature, access route
### Injury: cytopathic, immune, vascular, systemic
### Enveloped viruses fragile outside the body
### Non-enveloped capsids spread by surfaces, faecal-oral
### Tendencies, not sufficient infection-control rules

## Viral variation and selection
### Mutation, recombination, reassortment
### Most mutations neutral or harmful
### Selection enriches reproducing, transmitting variants
### Bottleneck passes a small subset
### Immune escape may compromise another property
### No intention, no guaranteed attenuation

## Fungal structure and dimorphism
### Chitin and glucan wall, ergosterol membrane
### Azoles, polyenes, echinocandins
#### Activity varies by species and site
### Yeast in non-sterile sample may be colonisation
#### Recovery from blood usually significant
### Invading hyphae prove disease more strongly
### Neutropenia: angioinvasion with little inflammation
### Dimorphic: mould outside, yeast after inhalation
#### Geography and exposure are part of microbiology
#### Impaired cellular immunity permits late dissemination

## Parasite life cycles
### Malaria: liver first, blood stage causes fever
#### Dormant liver forms cause relapse
#### Blood-stage treatment alone incomplete
### Thick films detect, thin films assess species
### Helminths: obstruction, migration, blood loss
### Eosinophilia tracks tissue migration
### Strongyloides autoinfection persists for decades
#### Steroids trigger dissemination and sepsis
#### Take exposure history before immune therapy
### Schistosome eggs drive granuloma and fibrosis

## Resistance and antimicrobial treatment
### Heritable variation meets selection and spread
### Target mutation, enzymes, pumps, permeability
### Horizontal transfer moves mechanisms together
### Genotype and phenotype are not identical
### Inhibitory concentration does not measure eradication
### Empirical choice: site, severity, host, history
### Narrow to the most limited effective option
### Duration matched to source control and response

## Specimens, microbiome, and causation
### Specimen quality limits what results show
#### Squamous-rich sputum misses lower airway
### Blood cultures before antibiotics without delay
### Microbiome provides colonisation resistance
### Dysbiosis is descriptive, not causal
### Distinguish presence from pathogenic action
#### Site, burden, invasion, timing strengthen cause
### Report method, site, burden, susceptibility
### Non-improvement: re-examine, do not escalate
