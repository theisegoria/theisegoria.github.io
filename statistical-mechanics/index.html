Statistical mechanics: microstates and temperature

Boltzmann weights, partition functions, and random walks.

Before you begin: Probability and energy

1. Boltzmann weights2. Partition function3. Random walk and diffusion

Predict, manipulate, then check your reasoning against the example and question. Graphs illustrate the mathematics; they do not replace a proof.

1. Boltzmann weights

Raise \(T\) and watch high-energy states become more probable.

Worked example. The partition function \(Z\) normalizes all state weights.

Watch out. The two-level model omits interactions and degeneracy.

What happens as \(T\) approaches zero?

The lowest-energy state dominates.

Reference: OpenStax · University Physics, Volume 2

2. Partition function

Compare free-energy changes as the number of accessible states grows.

Worked example. Adding accessible states lowers the free energy at fixed temperature.

Watch out. The state list is finite and chosen for visualization.

Why does multiplicity matter?

More microstates contribute more terms to Z.

Reference: OpenStax · University Physics, Volume 2

3. Random walk and diffusion

Increase the number of steps \(N\) and inspect the spreading distribution.

Worked example. The root-mean-square displacement grows like \(\sqrt{N}\).

Watch out. One realization fluctuates around the ensemble scaling law.

What grows linearly with \(N\)?

The mean-square displacement, not the displacement itself.

Reference: OpenStax · University Physics, Volume 2
