Interactive derivative geometry

Three arrays,
three jobs.

I read the Jacobian as the thing that moves tiny vectors, the Wronskian as the area spanned by solution states, and the Hessian as the record of how slope itself changes.

The unifying ideaI group the three together because each packages derivatives into linear algebra so geometry can do part of the reasoning.

Jacobian

The best local linear map

For F: ℝⁿ → ℝᵐ, the entry in row i and column j is ∂Fᵢ/∂xⱼ. Thus DF(p)h predicts the output change produced by a small input displacement h.

F(p + h) = F(p) + DF(p)h + o(‖h‖)

When the matrix is square, det DF gives signed local volume scale. A zero determinant means at least one infinitesimal direction has collapsed.

Wronskian

Area in state space

For two scalar functions, W = y₁y₂′ − y₂y₁′ is the signed area spanned by their state vectors (y, y′). I teach that picture before the determinant formula, because it is the one that survives once the algebra is forgotten. Nonzero W at one point certifies independence.

The caveat I always flag

Zero is not always a converse

For arbitrary differentiable functions, W ≡ 0 need not imply dependence. For solutions of the same regular linear ODE, Abel's identity restores the all-or-nothing conclusion.

Hessian

Directional curvature

For a scalar field f, Hᶠ contains every second partial derivative. In a unit direction u, the second directional derivative is uᵀHᶠu.

Classification

Read the eigenvalue signs

At a critical point: two positive eigenvalues mean a local minimum; two negative a maximum; mixed signs a saddle. A zero eigenvalue makes the quadratic test inconclusive.

The habits I drill

Keep the indexing visible

  • Jacobian: outputs label rows, inputs label columns.
  • Wronskian: choose a consistent order for functions and derivative rows.
  • Hessian: mixed partials should agree when f is C²; asymmetry is usually an algebra error.
  • Always interpret a determinant with its domain, codomain, and units.

The Hessian as a surface

Here the quadratic model q(u, v) = ½(au² + 2buv + cv²) is drawn as the surface it really is. A cut along any direction is a parabola whose curvature at the origin is uᵀHu; the green and violet curves along the eigen-directions give the largest and smallest of those curvatures.

Practice

Practise the moves that work for any Jacobian, Wronskian or Hessian. Questions are generated fresh at three levels.

The Jacobian as a local map

For F(x, y) = (x² − y, xy + y²), what is det DF at (1, 2)?

DF = [[2x, −1], [y, x + 2y]], so DF(1, 2) = [[2, −1], [2, 5]] and det = 10 + 2 = 12.

The Wronskian as state-space area

What is W(cos t, sin t)?

W = cos t · cos t − sin t · (−sin t) = 1, never zero.

The Hessian as curvature

At a critical point H = [[2, 0], [0, −3]]. What does the second-derivative test say?

The eigenvalues 2 and −3 have opposite signs, so it is a saddle.
