Optics: rays, waves, and colour

Lenses, interference, and polarization.

Before you begin: Waves and geometry

1. Thin lens2. Double-slit interference3. Polarization

Predict, manipulate, then check your reasoning against the example and question. Graphs illustrate the mathematics; they do not replace a proof.

1. Thin lens

Move the object and watch the image switch from real to virtual.

Worked example. An object beyond 2f produces an inverted reduced image.

Watch out. The thin-lens model ignores thickness and aberration.

What happens at d_o=f?

The image distance diverges: rays leave parallel.

Reference: OpenStax · University Physics, Volume 3

2. Double-slit interference

Change slit spacing and wavelength to move bright fringes across the screen.

Worked example. Bright fringes satisfy a path difference of mλ.

Watch out. The small-angle screen approximation is used in the plot.

What widens the fringe spacing?

A longer wavelength or smaller slit separation.

Reference: OpenStax · University Physics, Volume 3

3. Polarization

Rotate an analyser and compare transmitted intensity with Malus’s law.

Worked example. Crossed polarisers transmit no ideal light.

Watch out. Real filters have finite extinction and wavelength dependence.

Why does intensity depend on angle twice?

The electric-field amplitude projects once, then intensity squares amplitude.

Reference: OpenStax · University Physics, Volume 3
