Calculus of variations: paths and principles

Extremal paths, action, and geodesics.

Before you begin: Calculus and mechanics

Predict, manipulate, then check your reasoning against the example and question. Graphs illustrate the mathematics; they do not replace a proof.

1. Euler–Lagrange

Vary a trial path and compare its action with the stationary path.

Worked example. For L=½m q̇²−V(q), the equation is Newton’s law.

Watch out. Stationary does not always mean a global minimum.

What is being varied?

The whole path q(t), while endpoints are held fixed.

Reference: University of Cambridge · Calculus of Variations

2. Geodesics on a surface

Flatten a curved metric and compare coordinate lines with shortest paths.

Worked example. Great circles are geodesics on a sphere.

Watch out. A coordinate straight line need not be geometrically straight.

Why do geodesics look curved in a map?

Projection distorts distances and angles.

Reference: University of Cambridge · Calculus of Variations

3. Least action

Compare paths with the same endpoints and watch the classical path emerge as the stationary one.

Worked example. The classical path is a stationary-phase path in the semiclassical picture.

Watch out. The phrase least action is shorthand; the action is stationary, not always least.

What stays fixed in the variation?

The endpoints and the time interval.
