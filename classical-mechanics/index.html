Classical mechanics: motion and forces

Projectiles, oscillators, and orbital motion.

Before you begin: Vectors and calculus

1. Projectile motion2. Harmonic oscillator3. Orbital motion

Predict, manipulate, then check your reasoning against the example and question. Graphs illustrate the mathematics; they do not replace a proof.

1. Projectile motion

Change launch angle and inspect the trajectory, range, and conserved horizontal velocity.

Worked example. At 45° without drag, range is maximal for fixed speed.

Watch out. The model neglects air resistance and Earth curvature.

What remains constant?

Horizontal velocity, while vertical velocity changes by gravity.

Reference: OpenStax · University Physics, Volume 1

2. Harmonic oscillator

Tune damping and stiffness to move between underdamped, critical, and overdamped motion.

Worked example. Critical damping returns fastest without overshoot.

Watch out. The spring is linear and the mass is point-like.

What sets the regime?

The discriminant c²−4mk.

Reference: OpenStax · University Physics, Volume 1

3. Orbital motion

Change tangential speed and see circular, elliptical, or escaping paths in a teaching model.

Worked example. At circular speed, radius and speed remain constant.

Watch out. The two-body model omits perturbations and relativistic corrections.

What is conserved?

In the ideal model, energy and angular momentum.

Reference: OpenStax · University Physics, Volume 1
