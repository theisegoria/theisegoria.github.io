Fluid dynamics: flow, pressure, and vorticity

Streamlines, Bernoulli pressure, and rotating flow.

Before you begin: Vectors and calculus

1. Streamlines2. Bernoulli along a streamline3. Vorticity and circulation

Predict, manipulate, then check your reasoning against the example and question. Graphs illustrate the mathematics; they do not replace a proof.

1. Streamlines

Change the source and sink strengths and inspect the instantaneous velocity field.

Worked example. A streamline is tangent to the velocity field at every point.

Watch out. A streamline is not necessarily the path of one marked fluid parcel in unsteady flow.

What does an arrow encode?

The local velocity direction and relative speed.

Reference: MIT OpenCourseWare · Fluid Dynamics

2. Bernoulli along a streamline

Narrow a channel and compare speed, pressure, and elevation along a streamline.

Worked example. At equal height, faster flow corresponds to lower static pressure.

Watch out. The ideal relation assumes steady, incompressible, inviscid flow along a streamline.

Which term rises in a constriction?

The kinetic term ½ρv² as speed increases.

Reference: MIT OpenCourseWare · Fluid Dynamics

3. Vorticity and circulation

Rotate a local velocity field and compare vorticity with circulation around a small loop.

Worked example. Solid-body rotation has uniform vorticity twice the angular speed.

Watch out. A streamline pattern alone does not determine vorticity without velocity magnitudes.

What does circulation measure?

The line integral of velocity around the closed loop.

Reference: MIT OpenCourseWare · Fluid Dynamics
