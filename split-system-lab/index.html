THE USEFUL DIFFERENCE

It transfers more heat
than the electricity it uses.

A resistance heater turns 1 kWh of electricity into about 1 kWh of room heat. At a heating coefficient of performance (COP) of 4, a heat pump delivers 4 kWh: 1 from electricity and 3 collected outdoors. It does not create energy. [1]

04 / THE REAL-WORLD TRADE-OFFS

Efficient by design.
Better with the right home.

01

Heat and cool with one installation

A reverse-cycle model handles both seasons. “Split” describes the separate indoor and outdoor units; cooling-only split systems also exist.

02

Condition the space you use

A wall unit serves a room or connected zone without distribution ducts. Closed doors and distant rooms limit its reach. Multi-splits connect several indoor units to one outdoor unit.

03

Match output to demand

Inverter compressors can vary their speed as demand changes. Correct sizing and installation matter: a high nameplate capacity is not a guarantee of low running cost.

04

Weather changes the result

Heating efficiency and capacity can fall in cold weather. Frost may require a defrost cycle, temporarily interrupting heating. Use the climate zone on the Australian energy label.

05

Reduce the load first

Insulation, draught control and shading reduce the work required. Modest thermostat settings, clean filters and unobstructed coils help maintain performance.

06

Count the whole installation

Include electrical work, mounting, pipe runs and condensate drainage in a quote. Consider outdoor noise and location. Ordinary split systems recirculate indoor air; they do not replace ventilation.
