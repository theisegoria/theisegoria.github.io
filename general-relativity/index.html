General relativity: curvature, clocks, and light

Before you begin: Special relativity and differential geometry

Predict, manipulate, then check your reasoning against the example and question. Graphs illustrate the mathematics; they do not replace a proof.

1. An embedding diagram

Change the Schwarzschild radius and inspect a radial embedding profile. This is a spatial analogy, not a literal picture of spacetime.

Worked example. The surface becomes steep near the horizon in this two-dimensional embedding.

Watch out. An embedding diagram adds an auxiliary Euclidean dimension and does not show time curvature.

What is the extra vertical axis?

It is a visualization coordinate used to display intrinsic spatial geometry.

Reference: Einstein Online · General Relativity

2. Gravitational time dilation

Move a clock outward from the horizon and compare its proper time with a distant reference clock.

Worked example. The lapse tends to zero as r approaches r_s and tends to one far away.

Watch out. The formula is for a static clock outside a nonrotating spherical mass.

Which clock accumulates less proper time?

The clock deeper in the gravitational field.

Reference: Einstein Online · General Relativity

3. Weak-field light deflection

Change the impact parameter and compare a bent ray with the weak-field estimate.

Worked example. The estimate is valid when the impact parameter is large compared with r_s.

Watch out. Near the photon sphere, the weak-field approximation fails dramatically.

What reduces the deflection?

Increasing the impact parameter b.
