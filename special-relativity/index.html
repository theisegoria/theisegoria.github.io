Special relativity: space, time, and light

Lorentz diagrams, time dilation, and invariant intervals.

Before you begin: Algebra and geometry

1. Lorentz diagrams2. Proper time3. Relativistic momentum

Predict, manipulate, then check your reasoning against the example and question. Graphs illustrate the mathematics; they do not replace a proof.

1. Lorentz diagrams

Change relative speed and see how the coordinate grid tilts while light rays remain at 45°.

Worked example. The light cone is invariant under a Lorentz boost.

Watch out. The diagram uses units with c=1.

What stays fixed for all inertial observers?

The speed of light and the spacetime interval.

Reference: Einstein Online · Special Relativity

2. Proper time

Compare a moving clock with the coordinate clock and inspect time dilation.

Worked example. A moving clock accumulates less proper time between the same events.

Watch out. The effect is reciprocal between inertial frames; acceleration changes the comparison setup.

What happens as v approaches c?

The proper-time factor approaches zero.

Reference: Einstein Online · Special Relativity

3. Relativistic momentum

Increase speed and compare Newtonian momentum with the relativistic correction.

Worked example. At low speed γ≈1; near c the correction dominates.

Watch out. Mass-energy units and c=1 are used for the plot.

Why can a massive object not reach c?

γ diverges, requiring unbounded energy.

Reference: Einstein Online · Special Relativity
