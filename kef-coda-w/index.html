THE ACOUSTIC IDEA

Bring the origins
of sound together.

In a conventional two-way layout, the tweeter and bass/midrange driver occupy different positions. Around the frequencies they share, moving above or below the speaker changes the distance from each driver to your ears.

Uni-Q aims to bring their acoustic origins together. The surrounding cone also forms part of the tweeter’s waveguide. The intent is a smoother transition as you move away from the central listening position. KEF’s Uni-Q explanation ↗

Coincidence does not make a speaker omnidirectional, remove room reflections, or guarantee identical sound in every seat. Real performance still depends on driver directivity, crossover design, cabinet diffraction and the room.

EVIDENCE / SCOPE

What this model shows.

Product specifications and architectural claims were checked against the primary sources listed here; acoustic performance was not independently measured. The speaker view uses KEF’s own Coda W GLB from its product page, in its supplied finish. The driver view is a section reconstructed from KEF’s published descriptions of the Uni-Q architecture, not a teardown or a manufacturing drawing; dimensions KEF does not publish are representative. The path illustration is an idealised calculation, not a validated simulation or measured response.

KEF lists Coda W as 12th-generation Uni-Q; this study does not assume it includes MAT. KEF distinguishes 12th-generation Uni-Q from the later version with Metamaterial Absorption Technology, so the duct here ends in a plain damped chamber rather than MAT.
