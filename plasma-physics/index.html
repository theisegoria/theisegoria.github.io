Plasma physics: screening, orbits, and waves

Explore Debye screening, charged-particle gyromotion, and a cold-plasma dispersion sketch.

Before you begin: Electromagnetism and differential equations

Predict, manipulate, then check your reasoning against the example and question. Graphs illustrate the mathematics; they do not replace a proof.

1. Debye screening

Change the Debye length and inspect how a test charge potential decays through a plasma.

Worked example. Larger \(\lambda_D\) makes the screened field persist farther from the charge.

Watch out. The spherical Coulomb prefactor and the linearized screening model are idealizations.

What sets the screening distance?

The Debye length \(\lambda_D\).

Reference: MIT OpenCourseWare · Plasma Physics

2. Cyclotron motion

Change the signed charge-to-mass ratio and watch the planar orbit reverse its sense of rotation.

Worked example. The orbit radius shrinks as the magnetic field or charge-to-mass magnitude grows.

Watch out. The animation is a uniform-field, nonrelativistic slice with no collisions.

What reverses when q changes sign?

The direction of gyromotion, while the radius uses |q|.

Reference: MIT OpenCourseWare · Plasma Physics

3. Plasma-wave dispersion

Compare the electromagnetic branch with the plasma-frequency cutoff in a compact dispersion diagram.

Worked example. Below \(\omega_p\), an electromagnetic wave cannot propagate in this cold, unmagnetized model.

Watch out. Temperature, collisions, and a magnetic field add branches omitted from this teaching sketch.

What happens at \(k=0\)?

The branch begins at the plasma frequency \(\omega_p\).

Reference: MIT OpenCourseWare · Plasma Physics
