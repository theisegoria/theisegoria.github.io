Functional analysis: norms, projections, and operators

Compare norms, orthogonal projections, and the spectrum of a small operator.

Before you begin: Linear algebra and real analysis

1. Norms and unit balls2. Orthogonal projection3. Operator spectrum

Predict, manipulate, then check your reasoning against the example and question. Graphs illustrate the mathematics; they do not replace a proof.

1. Norms and unit balls

Change p and watch the unit ball interpolate between familiar geometries.

Worked example. For \(p=2\), the unit ball is the Euclidean circle.

Watch out. The limit \(p=\infty\) is shown by a bounded approximation to the maximum norm.

Which unit ball has the sharpest corners?

The \(p=1\) diamond.

Reference: MIT OpenCourseWare · Functional Analysis

2. Orthogonal projection

Move a vector and inspect its projection onto a chosen direction in an inner-product space.

Worked example. The residual is orthogonal to the subspace: \(\langle v-\operatorname{proj}_u v,u\rangle=0\).

Watch out. Projection depends on the chosen inner product, not only on the drawn direction.

What vanishes at the projection?

The inner product of the residual with the direction.

Reference: MIT OpenCourseWare · Functional Analysis

3. Operator spectrum

Vary a two-dimensional operator and compare its eigenvalue markers with repeated action on a vector.

Worked example. For a diagonal operator, the coordinate axes reveal the eigenvectors directly.

Watch out. A finite matrix spectrum is a teaching proxy for the richer spectra of infinite-dimensional operators.

What happens when an eigenvalue crosses zero?

The operator loses invertibility along that eigendirection.

Reference: MIT OpenCourseWare · Functional Analysis
